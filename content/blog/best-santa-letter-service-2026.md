If you landed here after searching for the Macy's Santa letter program: it was discontinued in 2023. ## The free DIY option: USPS North Pole postmark

Before spending money anywhere, it's worth knowing this exists. The U.S. Postal Service runs an annual program where you write a personalized Santa response yourself, mail it through a specific postmark process in Anchorage, Alaska, and your child receives it with a real North Pole postmark and Santa return address.

It's free, genuinely magical, and completely under your control. The letter says exactly what you want it to say. The tradeoff is effort: it requires planning ahead, meeting a firm early December deadline, and writing the letter yourself.

## Service-by-service breakdown

### Santa Claus House (North Pole, Alaska)

**What it is:** The original. Santa Claus House has been sending physical letters from North Pole, Alaska since 1952, which gives them something no newer service can match: a real postmark from an actual town called North Pole, a genuine street address, and a stationery design that's been largely unchanged for decades. The package includes the letter on that distinctive stationery, a "Good List" sticker, a "Santa Dollar," and a Santa photo.

**What it does well:** The provenance is real. North Pole, Alaska is a real place, and letters genuinely mail from there. For families where the physical authenticity of the postmark matters, this is the strongest option. It's also been around long enough that parents who received one as children are now ordering them for their own kids.

**What it doesn't do as well:** The personalization is limited compared to newer services. You provide basic details, but the letter content follows a template that hasn't changed much over the years. There's no instant delivery option. And the website, while functional, is not a modern ordering experience.

**Pricing:** Not confirmed from current research. The last posted price found was around $5.95, but verify directly at santaclaushouse.com before ordering.

**Best for:** Families where the physical North Pole, Alaska postmark is the point, and grandparents ordering a keepsake-quality tradition for grandchildren.

### Package from Santa

**What it is:** A well-established physical mail service with four package tiers (Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum), each including a personalized letter plus varying combinations of extras: a Nice List certificate, Santa photo, holiday envelope artwork, and at the top tier, a Build-A-Bear plush.

**What it does well:** Package from Santa has been doing this long enough to have the physical product dialed in. The parchment-style paper, ribbon tying, and North Pole packaging are legitimately nice. The entry price is low, which makes it accessible. Customer reviews tend to be strong.

**What it doesn't do as well:** The extras can make this feel like upsell territory. The Bronze tier is a letter at $9.95, but the presentation that earns the good reviews is mostly in the Silver and Gold tiers. If you want the full package experience, plan to spend more than the entry price suggests. Also: this is a physical mail service with holiday-season logistics, meaning you're ordering weeks in advance and trusting the postal system.

**Pricing:** Starting at $9.95 (Bronze). Higher tiers vary. Check packagefromsanta.com for current pricing on each level.

**Best for:** Parents who want a physical, ribbon-tied letter in themed packaging and don't mind ordering well ahead of Christmas. Strong choice for younger kids who will be wowed by the presentation.

### Portable North Pole

**What it is:** A video service, not a letter service. PNP creates personalized Santa videos (and video calls) using your child's name, age, and a few details. There's a free tier for basic videos. Premium videos run around $5.99 per video as of 2026, and a subscription pass is available for more content.

**What it does well:** The reaction moment. Watching a child's face when Santa appears to be talking directly to them in a video is hard to replicate in print. PNP has been the dominant player in this category for years, and for a certain age range, it lands hard.

**What it doesn't do as well:** It's an experience, not a keepsake. A video is exciting in the moment and then largely forgotten, especially as kids get older. The letter format creates something a child can reread, save in a box, and find again at 19. PNP also doesn't offer a physical mail option.

**Pricing:** Free tier available. Premium videos at approximately $5.99 each; pass pricing varies. Check portablenorthpole.com for current offers.

**Best for:** Younger kids (roughly 3-7) where the visual magic of a talking Santa video will land. Less effective as a standalone gift for older kids or as a keepsake item. For a deeper comparison of PNP against a physical letter specifically, [Portable North Pole vs. a real Santa letter](/blog/portable-north-pole-vs-santa-letter) covers that decision in full.
