Ask ten parents when the deadline for a Santa letter is and you will get ten answers, and most of them will be right about something.

That is because there is no single Santa letter deadline. There is a USPS mailing date for letters you post yourself, a separate window for the North Pole postmark program, another date for letters written *to* Santa, and then whatever each online service sets for its own orders. They land weeks apart. Mixing them up is how a parent ends up in the second week of December holding a letter that will not arrive until after the tree comes down.

This post lays out every 2026 date that matters, where each one comes from, and a simple calendar for when to act.

## USPS holiday mailing dates for 2026

On September 22, the Postal Service published its [recommended 2026 holiday mailing dates](https://about.usps.com/newsroom/national-releases/2026/0922-usps-recommends-2026-holiday-mailing-shipping-dates.htm). These matter if you are writing and mailing a Santa letter yourself, whether in a store-bought envelope or through a DIY postmark trick.

For the contiguous US (the lower 48 states), the recommended send-by dates for expected delivery before December 25 are:

- **USPS Ground Advantage:** Thursday, December 17
- **First-Class Mail:** Thursday, December 17
- **Priority Mail:** Friday, December 18
- **Priority Mail Express:** Saturday, December 19

For Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, and US territories, Ground Advantage moves one day earlier to Wednesday, December 16. First-Class Mail, Priority Mail, and Priority Mail Express keep the same dates as the lower 48.

A letter from Santa in a standard envelope travels as First-Class Mail, so December 17 is the date to know. Two things are worth saying about it.

First, these are recommended dates, not guarantees. USPS describes them as the dates for *expected* delivery before December 25. Mailing a few days ahead of the recommended date gives you a buffer if anything slows down in the busiest week of the year.

Second, the date is the day it enters the mail, not the day you write it. If you drop a letter in a collection box after the last pickup, it goes out the next day.

For military and international mail, USPS said in its announcement that the complete list of suggested dates would be available on its [holiday shipping page](https://www.usps.com/holiday/holiday-shipping-dates.htm) beginning in mid-October. That page is the definitive source, and it is worth checking directly before you mail anything to an APO, FPO, or DPO address or overseas.

## The two USPS Santa programs, and their very different dates

The Postal Service runs two Santa programs, and their deadlines are easy to confuse because both involve Santa and both involve the post office.

**The North Pole postmark program** is the one where you write Santa's letter yourself, and USPS sends it back to your child with a North Pole postmark. The [official program page](https://about.usps.com/holidaynews/letters-from-santa.htm) recommends sending your letters between November 23 and December 1. That window is early for a reason: your envelope has to reach Anchorage, get postmarked, and then travel back to your child. We have a full [North Pole postmark guide](https://www.santasletter.ai/blog/north-pole-postmark-2026-guide) with the exact two-envelope setup and the mistakes that cause letters to go missing.

**USPS Operation Santa** runs in the other direction. It is for letters your child writes *to* Santa, which volunteers can adopt and answer with gifts. According to the [Operation Santa program site](https://www.uspsoperationsanta.com/faqs), 2026 letters could be sent starting Monday, September 14, and they must be postmarked by Saturday, December 5. Adopters can register starting November 2, letter adoption begins November 16, and the last day to adopt a letter is December 12.

If your goal is for your child to *receive* a letter from Santa, the postmark program is the one you want. Operation Santa is for sending one.

## Online Santa letter services

Paid services set their own schedules, and they publish them in different levels of detail. Here is what the two long-running mailed services say on their own sites as of this writing.

**Santa Claus House** (North Pole, Alaska) says in its [Santa letter FAQ](https://www.santaclaushouse.com/santaletter_faq.asp) that to ensure Christmas delivery it must receive your order by December 10 for standard US shipping. It mails letters beginning the last week of November, and says orders placed after December 1 are usually mailed within three to five days. It asks you to allow up to seven to ten days after processing for the letter to arrive. The FAQ presents this as its standing policy rather than a 2026-specific date.

**Package from Santa** says on its [FAQ page](https://packagefromsanta.com/pages/faq) that personalization opens October 7 and shipping begins November 16, 2026, with packages going out within 48 to 72 hours of personalizing. Its FAQ does not list an order-by date for Christmas delivery, so if you are ordering late, it is worth asking them directly.

**Video and digital services** such as Portable North Pole deliver online, so there is no shipping deadline at all. The trade-off is that there is nothing in the mailbox. ## A simple calendar for when to order

Put together, the year looks like this.

**October to mid-November.** The unhurried window. Order a mailed letter and pick your posting date now, and the timing is handled. If you are doing the North Pole postmark yourself, write the letter and address both envelopes, so it is ready when the window opens.

**November 23 to December 1.** Mail your North Pole postmark envelope to Anchorage.

**By December 5.** If your child wrote a letter to Santa for Operation Santa, it needs its postmark.

**By December 8.** The latest posting date we suggest for a mailed letter from us to a US address.

**By December 10.** The Santa Claus House order cutoff for standard US shipping, according to its FAQ.

**By December 17.** The last USPS recommended date for First-Class Mail in the lower 48, if you are mailing a letter you wrote yourself.

**After December 17.** Switch to something that does not depend on the mail: a printed PDF you can hand-deliver, a letter left under the tree, or a video.

The pattern is simple. The more of the process that happens in someone else's building, the earlier you need to start. A letter you print at home has no deadline. A letter that has to travel to Anchorage and back needs its own window in late November.
